# Lifecycle Operating Model

## Purpose

Create a shared vocabulary for customer progression and a consistent method for deciding what should happen next.

## Reusable lifecycle stages

Adapt the labels to the business while preserving the logic:

1. Find and acquire
2. Convert and activate
3. Onboard and adopt
4. Engage and deepen
5. Expand and upgrade
6. Retain and protect
7. Win back or sunset

Stages are an executive and portfolio view. Operational decisions should use customer states and transitions.

## State-transition principle

Every program must name:

- current state
- desired next state
- customer benefit of the transition
- observable entry signal
- success signal
- exit and suppression conditions
- measurement design

Prefer achievable adjacent-state transitions over unrealistic jumps.

## Opportunity framing

Prioritize a transition using:

- reachable population
- customer benefit
- business benefit
- evidence strength
- feasibility
- time to learn
- treatment cost
- customer risk
- operational risk

When numeric inputs are unavailable, use qualitative ratings and label them as assumptions.

## Commercial-funnel connection

The conversion event should become the entry event for onboarding. Acquisition, conversion, post-purchase, retention, and reactivation should share identity, state, and suppression logic.

## Required output

A lifecycle map should include:

- stage name and purpose
- entry and exit criteria
- key customer behaviors
- healthy-state indicators
- risk indicators
- primary transitions
- existing journeys
- missing coverage
- owner
- data dependencies
- decisions required
