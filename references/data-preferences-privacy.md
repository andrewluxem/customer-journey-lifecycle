# Data, Preferences, and Privacy

## Identity journey

**anonymous → identified → progressively understood → decisionable**

## Data principles

- collect the minimum useful information
- provide a clear value exchange
- enrich progressively
- separate explicit data from inferred behavior
- use collected data promptly
- do not collect unused attributes
- provide correction and deletion paths
- honor consent and channel permissions
- avoid invasive personalization

## Explicit data

Examples include:

- contact information
- location at an appropriate level
- product ownership
- role or customer type
- goals
- interests
- proficiency
- channel preference
- content preference
- frequency preference
- privacy and profiling choices

## Inferred data

Examples include:

- product or content interest
- engagement trend
- purchase intent
- recency and frequency
- next-best content or action
- risk trend
- likely channel preference

Label inference confidence and do not present inference as customer-declared fact.

## Preference-center requirements

Customers should be able to:

- manage channel permissions
- select communication types
- update interests
- reduce frequency
- understand data use
- control profiling where required
- correct or remove information

## Design gate

For every requested attribute, document:

- customer benefit
- operational use
- source
- retention period
- sensitivity
- access control
- correction method
- deletion or suppression behavior
